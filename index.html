      How to read the map

      Blue areas usually get too much rain during El Niño. Yellow to red areas get too little rain, more heat, fires or smoke.

      The darker the colour, the higher the level, from Watch to Severe. A level is set by the most serious thing happening in the area, not by how many reports there are: one emergency-level alert counts more than many minor ones. It combines how strongly El Niño usually hits the area, the severity of active alerts and official warnings, and how intense satellite-detected fires are (their heat output, not just how many hotspots there are).

      Click a city, region or country to see the hardest-hit places, alerts, warnings and what's coming for that area only.

      What this page does not say

      Not every flood or fire is caused by El Niño. Alerts show up here when they fall in places where El Niño raises the risk at this time of year.

      This is a guide, not an official warning. In an emergency, follow your local authorities.
